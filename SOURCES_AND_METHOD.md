# Sources and Method

## Scope

This is a curated, expanding bibliography of writing about military strategy and closely related strategic thought. It includes classic primary texts, official doctrine or professional military education publications, and scholarly analysis. It is not an exhaustive list of every relevant title or a claim to represent every country.

## Country of origin

“Country or tradition of origin” records the main intellectual, institutional, or historical tradition in which a work was produced. For ancient works, modern borders may not apply; the table uses a recognizable present-day geographic label with a historical qualifier where helpful. For comparative books or studies written abroad, the catalog distinguishes origin from subject country in the table.

An author's nationality alone does not determine a work's origin. Translations are recorded as editions of the source work, and the country of the translator or publisher does not replace the source tradition. For a study written outside the country it examines, the catalog identifies the author's or work's origin and labels the country studied separately, for example “United States (Iran-focused study).”

## Source hierarchy

Bibliographic details are checked against, in order of preference:

1. Publisher and official institutional pages
2. National library and university library catalogs
3. Reputable scholarly reviews or academic catalogs

Where sources disagree or editions are difficult to identify, the table identifies the specific edition where possible and marks uncertain fields as “not confirmed” or “not identified.” Readers should verify ISBNs against the exact format and language they intend to obtain.

## Summaries and copyright

Summaries in this repository are written by contributors in their own words. They describe a book's stated subject at a high level and are not copied from publisher blurbs. The repository links to sources and does not host full books or substantial copyrighted excerpts.

## Coverage goals

Future contributions can expand coverage across additional countries, languages, historical periods, and schools of thought. Inclusion is not a ranking, recommendation, or claim that the entries are equivalent in genre or influence.
